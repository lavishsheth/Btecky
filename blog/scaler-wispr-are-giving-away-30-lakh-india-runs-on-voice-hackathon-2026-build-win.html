🔥 What Is India Runs on Voice Hackathon 2026?

India Runs on Voice is a nationwide hackathon organised by Scaler and Wispr.

The main objective is to encourage developers to build voice-first applications that solve real-world problems for people across India.

Most software applications are designed around keyboards, screens, and English-language interfaces. However, many people are more comfortable speaking in their native languages than typing.

This hackathon challenges developers to think differently and create applications where voice is the primary way to interact with technology.

For example, imagine:

  • A farmer accessing useful information through voice commands in their regional language.
  • A shopkeeper maintaining daily accounts simply by speaking.
  • A student answering educational questions verbally.
  • An elderly person using an application without navigating complicated menus.
  • A citizen filing a public-service complaint through a voice-based interface.

These are examples of the kind of real-world problems that voice-first technology can help address.

The challenge is to create something practical, accessible, and useful for people in India.

💡 What Do You Need to Build?

The central theme of this hackathon is:

India Runs on Voice – Build voice-first tools for India.

You need to create an application where voice is the main way users interact with the product.

The hackathon provides five different tracks. You can select a problem area that interests you and develop a solution around it.

Let's understand each track.

1. Civic Services

<badge color="info">Track 01</badge>

The first track focuses on making public services more accessible through voice-based technology.

Many people struggle with complicated online forms, government portals, and digital processes.

You can build applications that help people access civic services through natural speech.

Example project ideas:

  • Voice-based grievance filing.
  • Voice-assisted public-service applications.
  • Regional-language complaint registration.
  • Voice navigation for public-service information.

What does this unlock?

Public services for people who may find it difficult to fill out traditional digital forms.

2. Healthcare

<badge color="info">Track 02</badge>

Healthcare is another important area where voice-first technology can make a difference.

Patients may feel more comfortable describing their symptoms in their mother tongue rather than typing medical information.

You can build tools that help healthcare professionals and patients communicate more easily.

Example project ideas:

  • Voice-based symptom intake.
  • Regional-language patient assistance.
  • Voice-enabled healthcare information systems.
  • Tools for recording patient descriptions.

What does this unlock?

Better access to healthcare interactions for patients who think and communicate in their mother tongue.

3. Education

<badge color="info">Track 03</badge>

Education is one of the most promising areas for voice-based applications.

Not every student has access to advanced digital learning tools or feels comfortable typing answers.

You can build applications that allow students to interact with educational content using their voice.

Example project ideas:

  • Spoken quizzes in regional languages.
  • Voice-based learning assistants.
  • Interactive educational tools.
  • Spoken practice exercises for students.

What does this unlock?

More accessible learning experiences, especially for first-generation students.

4. Small Business

<badge color="info">Track 04</badge>

Small businesses and local shopkeepers often need simple tools to manage everyday activities.

A voice-based application can make these tasks easier without requiring complicated data entry.

Example project ideas:

  • Voice bookkeeping for kirana stores.
  • Daily sales recording through speech.
  • Voice-based inventory management.
  • Regional-language business assistants.

What does this unlock?

Practical digital tools for India's small businesses and kirana stores.

5. Everyday Access

<badge color="info">Track 05</badge>

The fifth track focuses on making everyday technology easier to access.

Older people and people with disabilities may face difficulties using applications that depend heavily on typing and complicated interfaces.

Voice-first tools can help make everyday digital activities more accessible.

Example project ideas:

  • Hands-free applications for older users.
  • Voice-based reminders.
  • Spoken navigation tools.
  • Accessible voice assistants.

What does this unlock?

Greater independence for elderly users and people with disabilities.

According to the official hackathon FAQ, the eligibility requirements are:

  • No previous AI experience is required.
  • No previous voice technology experience is required.

This means you don't need to be an experienced AI engineer to get started. A strong understanding of the problem, a practical idea, and a working solution are important starting points.

Can Beginners Participate?

Yes! The official FAQ specifically states that prior AI or voice experience is not required.

If you are familiar with programming, web development, Python, APIs, or AI tools, you can use those skills to start building your solution.

The most important thing is to identify a genuine problem and create something useful for the intended users.

A key part of this hackathon is building hands-free with Wispr Flow.

Wispr Flow is a voice-to-text AI tool that converts speech into clear, polished writing across applications.

The hackathon encourages participants to use voice as a primary way of interacting with their tools during the building process.

You can think about your development workflow in the following way:

  1. Speak your idea instead of typing every instruction.
  2. Use voice to communicate your development requirements.
  3. Explore the technical approach for your application.

Tips for Building a Strong Project

  • Focus on one genuine user problem.
  • Make voice the main interaction method, not just an optional feature.
  • Think about regional languages and different accents.
  • Keep the user experience simple.
  • Test your application with realistic conversations.
  • Make sure the application handles misunderstood speech gracefully.
  • Explain the real-world impact of your solution.

A small application that solves a genuine problem can be more useful than a complicated project without a clear purpose.
